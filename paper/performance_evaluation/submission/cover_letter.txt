Dear Editor,

Please find enclosed our manuscript, "When Does LLM-Serving Scheduler Adaptation Matter? Action Opportunity and Causal Headroom in Production-Derived Replay," for consideration as an Original research article in Performance Evaluation.

This manuscript evaluates a foundational premise of LLM-serving schedulers: before proposing complex adaptive mechanisms, does a strong default scheduler possess a genuinely useful alternative action? Rather than introducing a new system, we contribute a trace-driven performance-evaluation methodology that isolates canonical action opportunity from deployed measurement noise. Through a sequence of rigorous workload replays—spanning Azure and BurstGPT traces—we demonstrate that resource pressure must first create executable alternatives, which must then yield causal latency headroom to justify adaptation. Our confirmatory causal study bounds this mean oracle headroom at 2.0 ms for our evaluated systems.

We believe this aligns strongly with the scope of Performance Evaluation by providing a systematic, statistical evaluation of computer-system scheduling behavior using production-derived traces.
