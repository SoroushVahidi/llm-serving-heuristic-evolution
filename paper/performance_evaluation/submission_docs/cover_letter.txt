Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "When Does LLM-Serving Scheduler Adaptation Matter? Action Opportunity and Causal Headroom in Production-Derived Replay," for publication in Performance Evaluation as an Original Research Article.

The manuscript studies a performance-evaluation question that arises before building an adaptive LLM-serving scheduler: does a fixed scheduler reference encounter executable alternative actions, and do those one-step alternatives causally improve latency under the evaluated workload and resource regime? The paper answers this question through deterministic replay of production-derived traces, a simple six-policy diagnostic portfolio, resource-pressure interventions, fresh-window causal evaluation, robustness analysis, and a bounded vLLM correspondence probe.

The principal result is that scheduler adaptation opportunity is real but sparse, resource-conditioned, and reference-dependent. Abundant-resource replay of about one million decision states is action-null. KV-cache and active-sequence caps create disagreement, whereas the evaluated arrival-scaling configurations remain lightly loaded. In 720 fresh Azure disagreement states, most states have positive one-step latency headroom, but the median effect is 0.20 ms and the total is concentrated in a small number of windows. BurstGPT does not transfer this fresh-window opportunity under the evaluated construction.

The contribution is methodological rather than a new scheduler or deployment claim. It shows how to separate score diversity from executable action diversity, disagreement from causal benefit, and mean headroom from its distribution before investing in adaptive selector design. This framing fits Performance Evaluation's scope in workload replay, scheduling/resource evaluation, causal measurement, and statistically bounded system-performance interpretation.

All code, frozen confirmatory artifacts, robustness outputs, correction documentation, and the claim manifest are available in the public GitHub repository and are archived in the published v1.1.0 Zenodo dataset (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22866983, concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22865293). Raw third-party traces are obtained from their upstream sources.

This manuscript is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. I am the sole author and the corresponding author.
